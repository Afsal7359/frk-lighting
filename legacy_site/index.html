Why solar street lighting makes sense

Conventional street lighting needs a trench, a cable and a connection to the supply before it gives any light. Solar lights need a foundation and a pole.

  • Quicker to install. No digging up roads, driveways or compound walls.
  • Nothing to pay monthly. The sun charges the battery every day.
  • Keeps working in power cuts. Each light is independent.
  • Safer from cable theft. There is no copper cable to steal.
  • Easy to relocate. If your layout changes, the light moves with it.

How a solar street light works

  1. Daytime: the panel collects sun

    The solar panel turns sunlight into electricity, even on bright cloudy days at a lower rate.

  2. The battery stores it

    A charge controller fills the battery and protects it from overcharging.

  3. At dusk the light switches on

    A sensor notices the dark and turns the LED on without any switch or timer.

  4. At dawn it switches off

    The cycle repeats every day. Many models can dim late at night to stretch battery life.

Questions we hear often

Can't find yours? Ask us directly.

Do solar lights work during the monsoon?

Yes, if the battery is sized for it. We size the system for several cloudy days in a row, so lights keep running through the rainy season. Read more.

How long do the batteries last?

It depends on the battery type, how deeply it is discharged and the heat it sits in. We quote the battery type and warranty terms in writing.

Do I need permission or an electricity connection?

No electricity connection is needed. For public roads you may need local body approval, and we can supply the documents you need.

How long does installation take?

Most jobs take a day or two for a small group of lights. Foundations need time to cure before poles go up.
