How to size a solar street light for your road

Most complaints about solar lights come down to one thing: the system was too small for the job. Here is how to avoid that.

Start with the road, not the lamp

Measure the road width, the distance between poles and the pole height. A narrow lane with poles every 25 metres needs far less light than a two-lane road with poles every 40.

Decide how long the lights must stay on

A light at full brightness for twelve hours needs a much bigger battery than one that dims to 30% after midnight. Ask whether the site really needs full output all night.

Plan for the worst weeks

Size the battery and panel for your cloudiest weeks, with several days of rain in a row, not for a sunny week.

Leave a margin

Batteries lose capacity over the years and panels collect dust. A margin now keeps the light working in year five.

Kerala

Do solar street lights work in the monsoon?

Yes, but only if the system is designed for it. Panels still charge under cloud, just more slowly, so the battery has to carry the light through several dull days.

What to ask your supplier

How many days of backup the battery gives with no sun, and whether the lamp can dim late at night to save power.

Keep panels clean and unshaded

Dust, bird droppings and the shade of a growing coconut palm can cut charging more than rain does. Check the panel surroundings before you choose a pole position.

Check the waterproofing

Ask for the ingress protection (IP) rating of the lamp and battery box. For Kerala, higher is better.

Buying guide

All-in-one or split? Picking a solar light type

Both turn sunlight into light. They differ in how easy they are to install and how easy they are to adjust later.

All-in-one

Panel, battery and lamp in one housing. Quick to install and neat, ideal for estate roads and paths.

Split-type

The panel sits on top and the battery is separate. You can enlarge the panel for cloudy locations and replace the battery alone when it ages.

A simple rule

Short hours and modest light needs: all-in-one. Long hours, wide roads or dull weather: split-type.

Checklist

What to prepare before you ask for a quote

Twenty minutes of preparation saves days of back and forth.

About the site

Location, road length and surface. A sketch with pole positions marked is better than a long description.

About shade

Trees and buildings that shade the panel in the morning or afternoon.

About use

Who uses the place and when. A car park that empties at 9 p.m. needs different light from a road used by night shifts.

About the ground

Soil type and whether concrete foundations are possible.
